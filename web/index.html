Floods got 13× more frequent. They didn't get longer.

NOAA counts the days a coastal gauge floods. It never measures how long each flood lasts. This does — 137 gauges, every year on record.

Why this exists. Across these 137 gauges a flood day means anything from just over an hour above the threshold to most of a day — and NOAA's published counts score every one of them the same. This measures the hours instead.

What these numbers are. Hours the water sat at or above NOAA's NOS minor flood threshold at the gauge — exposure duration, not road-closure time. A tide gauge measures one point in the water, not the street, and gauges systematically undercount street flooding. Flood-day counts are computed independently from raw hourly observations and match NOAA's published counts at 99% of station-years. Years with under 90% valid observations are excluded rather than counted as calm.
